01 / The foundation

The Case for God

Christianity makes claims about the nature of reality. These arguments explore whether those claims have philosophical, scientific, and historical support.

🌌
COSMOLOGY

The Contingency Argument

Everything that exists contingently depends on something beyond itself for its existence. Does reality ultimately require a necessary foundation?

⚛️
PHYSICS

The Beginning of the Universe

Modern cosmology describes an expanding universe with a finite hot, dense early phase. What does this tell us about the ultimate origin of physical reality?

🧬
BIOLOGY

The Origin of Life

Life depends on extraordinarily complex chemical systems. Researchers continue investigating how the first living systems emerged from nonliving chemistry.

🎯
FINE-TUNING

The Fine-Tuning Argument

Certain physical constants appear to fall within ranges compatible with complex structures and life. Is design a better explanation than chance or deeper physical necessity?

🧠
PHILOSOPHY

Consciousness

Why is there subjective experience? Can consciousness be fully explained through physical processes, or does it point toward something deeper?

⚖️
MORALITY

Objective Morality

Are some things genuinely right or wrong regardless of what society believes? What grounds objective moral values and obligations?

Intellectual honesty: These are philosophical arguments, not universally accepted scientific proofs. Each has serious objections. The goal is to examine the reasoning rather than pretend every question is settled.
02 / The historical question

Who was Jesus?

The central claim of Christianity is not simply that God exists. It is that God entered human history through Jesus Christ. That claim deserves historical examination.

📜

Historical Existence

The broad scholarly consensus is that Jesus of Nazareth existed as a historical person. The next question is what can reliably be established about his life.

✝️

The Crucifixion

Jesus' crucifixion under Pontius Pilate is one of the most widely accepted facts about his life among historians of early Christianity.

📖

Early Christian Beliefs

Paul's letters preserve early Christian claims about Jesus' death, resurrection, and appearances to followers. Their dating and historical significance are important topics.

🪦

The Empty Tomb

The empty-tomb tradition is central to many Christian arguments. Historians disagree about how confidently its historicity can be established.

👥

Resurrection Experiences

Early followers reported experiences they interpreted as encounters with the risen Jesus. What best explains these reports?

🔥

The Resurrection Claim

Christians interpret the early testimony as evidence that God raised Jesus from the dead. This is a historical and theological claim, not merely a philosophical one.

A crucial distinction: establishing that early Christians believed Jesus had risen is not identical to establishing that a supernatural resurrection occurred. The latter requires evaluating the competing explanations of the historical evidence.
03 / Christian theology

Understanding the Trinity

Christianity teaches that there is one God who exists eternally as three distinct persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

Ⅰ

The Father

The Father is fully God, not a separate god or a fraction of God.

Ⅱ

The Son

Jesus Christ is understood as fully divine and fully human in historic Christian theology.

Ⅲ

The Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is understood as a distinct divine person, fully sharing the one divine nature.

The Trinity does not teach that God is one person and three persons in the same sense. It distinguishes between one divine being and three persons. Whether this doctrine is coherent and biblically justified is an important theological discussion.

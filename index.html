01 / Does God exist?

The foundation of reality

Before asking whether Christianity is true, we should ask whether theism offers a better explanation of existence, the universe, consciousness, morality, and reason than its alternatives.

🌌
COSMOLOGY

Why is there something rather than nothing?

The universe exists. The deeper philosophical question is why contingent physical reality exists at all rather than nothing. A necessary foundation is one proposed explanation.

⚛️
PHYSICS

The universe and its origin

Modern cosmology describes an expanding universe with a hot, dense early state. The Big Bang model does not, by itself, settle whether the universe was created from absolute nothing.

🎯
FINE-TUNING

Why does the universe permit life?

Some physical parameters appear to lie within ranges compatible with complex structures. Design, physical necessity, and multiverse proposals are competing explanations.

🧠
CONSCIOUSNESS

Why is there subjective experience?

Brain activity correlates with conscious experience, but philosophers continue to debate how physical processes relate to first-person awareness.

⚖️
MORALITY

Are moral truths objective?

If some actions are genuinely wrong regardless of opinion, what makes them wrong? Theism offers one account; secular moral realism offers another.

🔭
REASON

Why trust human reasoning?

We rely on reason to evaluate every worldview, including naturalism and theism. The question is how best to explain the reliability and limits of human cognition.

How the case works: These arguments can support a cumulative case for theism. They do not independently prove that Jesus is God. That requires a separate examination of Jesus, the earliest Christian testimony, and the resurrection claim.
02 / The central claim

The case for Jesus Christ

Christianity stands or falls on claims about Jesus. The central question is not merely whether Jesus existed, but whether the best explanation of the evidence is that He truly rose from the dead and is who historic Christianity says He is.

📜
HISTORY

Jesus existed

The broad scholarly consensus is that Jesus of Nazareth was a real first-century Jewish teacher who was executed under Pontius Pilate. His existence is not the main historical controversy.

✝️
HISTORY

Jesus was crucified

Jesus' crucifixion is among the most widely accepted facts about his life. The early Christian movement centered its message on a crucified Messiah.

📖
EARLY TESTIMONY

Resurrection belief appeared early

Paul's letters preserve early traditions that Jesus died, was buried, was raised, and appeared to followers. First Corinthians 15 is especially important to this debate.

👥
HISTORY

Followers reported appearances

The earliest Christian sources report that individuals and groups experienced what they understood as appearances of the risen Jesus. The nature and explanation of these experiences remain debated.

🪦
HISTORICAL DEBATE

The empty tomb

The empty tomb is a major part of many Christian arguments. Scholars disagree about how confidently it can be established from the surviving sources.

🔥
THEOLOGY

The resurrection

Christians argue that God raised Jesus bodily from death. If true, this would be powerful evidence for Jesus' unique authority and identity. It is the central claim that competing explanations must address.

The key inference: Early belief in the resurrection is historically important, but it is not identical to proving that the resurrection occurred. The Christian case must argue that the resurrection best explains the relevant evidence, while addressing alternative explanations fairly.
THE CHRISTIAN ARGUMENT

Why the resurrection matters

If Jesus truly rose from the dead through God's action, Christianity is not merely one spiritual philosophy among many. It would mean that God publicly vindicated Jesus and that His teachings about His identity deserve extraordinary attention.

What would need to be established?

First, determine which facts about Jesus' death and the earliest resurrection claims are historically well supported. Second, compare explanations such as sincere religious experiences, legendary development, mistaken beliefs, and the Christian claim of a real resurrection. Third, explain why a supernatural explanation is or is not justified. The conclusion should follow from the evidence, not be assumed in advance.

THE IDENTITY OF JESUS

Did Jesus claim divine status?

Christians point to Jesus' authority, his relationship with the Father, his use of divine titles and imagery, and passages such as John 1, John 8:58, John 10:30, and Mark 14:61–64. The interpretation of these passages is debated, so each should be read in context.

Why the resurrection is relevant to this question

If Jesus made divine claims and God raised Him, Christians argue that the resurrection is divine vindication of His identity and mission. The argument requires both a careful case about what Jesus claimed and a careful case that the resurrection happened.

03 / Christian doctrine

One God. Three persons. One divine nature.

Historic Christianity teaches that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct persons who share the one divine nature. The doctrine is not that there are three gods.

Ⅰ

The Father

The Father is fully God. He is not the Son or the Holy Spirit.

Ⅱ

The Son

Jesus Christ is understood as fully divine and fully human. The Son is distinct from the Father.

Ⅲ

The Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is understood as a distinct divine person, not merely an impersonal force.

The Trinity distinguishes between what God is (one divine being) and who God is (three persons). Whether this formulation is philosophically coherent and biblically justified is a question that deserves detailed argument.
04 / Comparative religion

Testing competing worldviews

If Christianity is true, contradictory religious claims cannot all be true in the same sense. The way to assess them is to compare their central claims, historical foundations, internal coherence, and explanatory power—not to caricature their followers.

Important: This section evaluates claims, not the worth or intelligence of people who hold them. A strong Christian argument should represent each tradition accurately and engage its best thinkers.

"Elements made God."

"Energy can create itself."

Rebuttals:
  • Quantum Equations: Describing a quantum state's evolution doesn't explain why mathematical laws or the Hilbert space exist (Gödel / Hawking) or solve the measurement problem—and physics stops at the BGV boundary, making metaphysics necessary.

Question 13

"Jesus isn't God / God is merely within Jesus like a prophet."

Fallacy: Word-Concept Fallacy & Equivocation.
Rebuttal: John 1:1 says the Word WAS God and became flesh (John 1:14); Colossians 2:9 says the whole fullness of deity dwells in Him bodily; He claimed Yahweh's eternal name ("Before Abraham was, I AM", John 8:58), existed in glory before the world began (John 17:5), took Yahweh's exclusive title "First and Last" (Isaiah 44:6 vs. Revelation 1:17), and unlike prophets who refuse worship (Acts 14:15), Jesus accepted Thomas calling Him "My Lord and my God" (John 20:28).
Question 14

"If Jesus was born human, didn't He sin?"

Fallacy: False Equivalence & Non Sequitur.
Rebuttal: Conceived by the Holy Spirit without Adam's sin nature (Luke 1:35; Genesis 3:15), Jesus lived a 100% sinless life (Hebrews 4:15; 2 Corinthians 5:21; 1 Peter 2:22; 1 John 3:5), qualifying Him as the spotless Lamb of God to pay humanity's infinite sin debt.
Question 15

"The Bible was just written by men."

Fallacy: Genetic Fallacy.
Rebuttal: Human authors don't rule out a divine Author—God carried the writers along like wind fills a sail (2 Peter 1:21; 2 Timothy 3:16). The real question isn't who held the pen but whether the content is true—and its history, prophecy, and manuscripts check out (Axioms 14, 15).
Question 16

"The Bible has been changed and translated too many times."

Fallacy: False Analogy (the "Telephone Game").
Rebuttal: Modern Bibles aren't translations of translations—they're translated straight from Hebrew and Greek and checked against 5,800+ Greek manuscripts. When the Dead Sea Scrolls turned up, they showed Isaiah had been copied with remarkable accuracy across 1,000 years (Axiom 14).
Question 17

"Science has disproved God."

Fallacy: Category Error & Scientism.
Rebuttal: Science explains how nature works, not why there is a nature at all. "Only science gives truth" isn't a scientific finding—it refutes itself (Axiom 13). The founders of modern science—Kepler, Boyle, Newton, Faraday, Maxwell—believed in a rational Creator, which is exactly why they expected the universe to make sense (Axiom 3).
Question 18

"Evolution proves there's no God."

Fallacy: Non Sequitur.
Rebuttal: Evolution describes how living things change—it can't explain why the universe began (Axiom 1), where the laws of physics came from (Axiom 3), how the first self-replicating code arose (Axiom 4), or why matter became conscious (Axiom 6). A process still needs a Lawgiver.
Question 19

"If God is good, why is there so much evil and suffering?"

Fallacy: Stolen Concept.
Rebuttal: Calling anything "evil" assumes an objective standard of good—which only exists if God does (Axiom 7). Evil is a privation, not a creation (Axiom 9), and God allows real freedom because forced love isn't love. He didn't stay distant either: Jesus suffered with us, and He will wipe away every tear (Revelation 21:4; Romans 8:18).
Question 20

"God is just an imaginary sky daddy."

Fallacy: Ad Hominem (Mockery) & Begging the Question.
Rebuttal: Mockery isn't an argument. The case for God rests on evidence—the BGV boundary, fine-tuning, the DNA code, and the resurrection (Axioms 1, 4, 10, 11)—not wishful thinking. The real fairy tale is "nothing created everything."
Question 21

"Which God? There are thousands of gods."

Fallacy: False Equivalence.
Rebuttal: The question isn't "Is there some god?" but "Which worldview survives the evidence?" Finite gods like Zeus or Thor are part of the universe, not its uncaused Cause (Axiom 12). Run every option through the 20 axioms—only one is left standing.
Question 22

"You're only a Christian because of where you were born."

Fallacy: Genetic Fallacy.
Rebuttal: How someone came to a belief doesn't decide whether it's true—by that logic, the atheist is only an atheist because of where they were born. Christianity spread from Jerusalem into every culture on earth because of evidence, not geography (Axiom 20).
Question 23

"Jesus never even existed."

Fallacy: Denial of Historical Evidence.
Rebuttal: Virtually every historian—including skeptic Bart Ehrman (Did Jesus Exist?, 2012)—affirms Jesus lived and was crucified. Tacitus (Annals 15.44), Josephus (Antiquities 18.3.3; 20.9.1), and Paul's letters written within about 20–25 years confirm it (Axioms 10, 20).
Question 24

"Jesus is just a copy of Horus, Mithras, or other pagan gods."

Fallacy: Parallelomania (False Analogy).
Rebuttal: The "Horus was born of a virgin on December 25, had 12 disciples, and was crucified" claims come from modern books and internet videos, not ancient Egyptian sources. Most evidence for the Mithras cult comes after Christianity began. Even skeptic scholars like Bart Ehrman reject the copycat theory.
Question 25

"The disciples just hallucinated the resurrection."

Fallacy: Ad Hoc Hypothesis.
Rebuttal: Hallucinations are private, but Jesus appeared to more than 500 people at once (1 Corinthians 15:6), to skeptics like James and enemies like Paul—and hallucinations can't empty a tomb (Axiom 10).
Question 26

"The disciples stole the body."

Fallacy: Ad Hoc Hypothesis.
Rebuttal: Then they suffered and died for what they knew was a lie—liars make terrible martyrs. The stolen-body story itself admits the tomb was empty (Matthew 28:11–15), and it can't explain why Paul and James converted (Axioms 10, 20).
Question 27

"Jesus didn't really die—He just passed out."

Fallacy: Ad Hoc Hypothesis (Swoon Theory).
Rebuttal: Roman soldiers were professional executioners, a spear pierced His side (John 19:34), and a 1986 article in the Journal of the American Medical Association concluded He was dead. A half-dead, scourged man could never convince anyone He had conquered death (Axiom 10).
Question 28

"The Gospels contradict each other."

Fallacy: Confusing Differences with Contradictions.
Rebuttal: Independent eyewitnesses naturally report different details—identical stories would suggest collusion. One angel at the tomb (Matthew 28:2) and two (Luke 24:4) isn't a contradiction: where there are two, there's one. On the core facts—death, burial, empty tomb, appearances—all four agree.
Question 29

"The God of the Old Testament is evil and genocidal."

Fallacy: Presentism & Stolen Concept.
Rebuttal: Calling anything "evil" requires the objective standard only God provides (Axiom 7). The judgment on Canaan came after 400+ years of patience (Genesis 15:16) against cultures that burned their own children as sacrifices (Leviticus 18:21; Deuteronomy 12:31)—and God judged Israel by the same standard.
Question 30

"Hell is unfair—why punish finite sins forever?"

Fallacy: Category Error.
Rebuttal: The weight of a crime depends on who it's against—sin against an infinitely holy God is infinite (see the 4-Step Proof). Yet God doesn't want anyone to perish (2 Peter 3:9); He paid the debt Himself so no one has to (John 3:16–18).
Question 31

"What about people who never heard of Jesus?"

Fallacy: Red Herring.
Rebuttal: Everyone has creation and conscience as witnesses (Romans 1:20; 2:15), and the Judge of all the earth will do what is right (Genesis 18:25). God is sending the gospel to every nation (Matthew 28:19; Revelation 7:9). But you have heard—so the real question is what you will do with Jesus.
Question 32

"Faith means believing without evidence."

Fallacy: Equivocation.
Rebuttal: Biblical faith (pistis) is trust based on evidence. Jesus gave "many convincing proofs" (Acts 1:3), Paul pointed to living eyewitnesses (1 Corinthians 15:6), and Luke investigated everything carefully before writing (Luke 1:1–4). Believing the universe made itself from nothing takes far more blind faith (Axioms 1, 4).
Question 33

"Can God make a rock so heavy He can't lift it?"

Fallacy: Logical Contradiction (Category Error).
Rebuttal: Omnipotence means the power to do anything logically possible—not nonsense. "A rock too heavy for the all-powerful" is like a square circle: it isn't a thing at all (Axiom 13). Scripture even says God cannot lie (Titus 1:2)—that's perfection, not weakness.
Question 34

"The burden of proof is on you, not me."

Fallacy: Shifting the Burden of Proof.
Rebuttal: Everyone who makes a claim carries a burden—including "the universe came from nothing" and "life arose by chance." S-GUOT meets its burden with 20 axioms; atheism still owes an explanation for the universe's beginning, the DNA code, consciousness, and morality (Axioms 1, 4, 6, 7).
Question 35

"Christianity caused the Crusades and the Inquisition."

Fallacy: Tu Quoque & Equivocation (confusing man-made religion with Jesus).
Rebuttal: Jesus commanded love for enemies (Matthew 5:44) and rebuked violence in His name (Matthew 26:52); people disobeying Jesus don't refute Jesus. Meanwhile, officially atheist regimes under Stalin, Mao, and Pol Pot killed tens of millions in the 20th century (Axiom 19).
Question 36

"Religion is just a crutch for weak people."

Fallacy: Ad Hominem & Genetic Fallacy.
Rebuttal: Why someone believes doesn't decide whether it's true. And the first Christians gained no comfort from their faith—only prison, beatings, and execution (Axiom 20). If Jesus rose from the dead, He isn't a crutch; He's the cure.
Question 37

"Morality just comes from evolution or society."

Fallacy: Genetic Fallacy & Is–Ought Fallacy.
Rebuttal: Evolution and society can explain why people feel moral pressure—not why anything is objectively right or wrong. If morality is just herd instinct, the Holocaust wasn't evil, just unpopular. Objective moral duties need a Moral Lawgiver (Axioms 7, 17).
Question 38

"The universe came from a quantum vacuum—from nothing."

Fallacy: Equivocation (on "nothing").
Rebuttal: A quantum vacuum isn't nothing—it's a field with energy, governed by physical laws. Calling that "nothing" is a bait-and-switch, exactly the criticism philosopher of physics David Albert pressed against Lawrence Krauss's A Universe from Nothing (2012). Something still has to explain the field and its laws (Axioms 1, 3).
Question 39

"Aliens seeded life on Earth."

Fallacy: Infinite Regress (pushing the problem back).
Rebuttal: Directed panspermia (proposed by Francis Crick and Leslie Orgel in 1973) only moves the question back: who wrote the aliens' DNA? It concedes that life's information needs an intelligence (Axiom 4)—and the aliens still need an uncaused First Cause (Axiom 1).
Question 40

"Atheism is just a lack of belief."

Fallacy: Equivocation (redefining atheism).
Rebuttal: A rock "lacks belief" in God too. Anyone who says the universe, life, and mind exist without God holds a worldview that has to explain them (Axioms 1, 4, 6). Calling it a "lack of belief" is a way to dodge the burden of proof.
Question 41

"The Trinity is a contradiction—1 + 1 + 1 = 3."

Fallacy: Strawman & Category Error.
Rebuttal: The Trinity isn't three gods in one god. It's one What (one divine being) and three Whos (Father, Son, Spirit)—think 1 × 1 × 1 = 1. "One" and "three" refer to different things, so there's no contradiction (Matthew 28:19; 2 Corinthians 13:14; Axiom 8).
Question 42

"Jesus never said, 'I am God.'"

Fallacy: Argument from Silence.
Rebuttal: He said it the way a first-century Jew would: forgiving sins only God can forgive (Mark 2:5–7), claiming God's name (John 8:58), accepting worship (Matthew 14:33; John 20:28), and claiming equality with the Father (John 5:18; 10:30–33). His enemies picked up stones because they understood exactly what He meant.
Question 43

"Paul invented Christianity."

Fallacy: Historical Revisionism.
Rebuttal: Paul passed on what he had received (1 Corinthians 15:3), checked his gospel with Peter and James (Galatians 1:18–19; Galatians 2:9), and they gave him the right hand of fellowship. Jesus was worshiped as God from the start—Philippians 2:5–11 preserves an early hymn about it.
Question 44

"The Bible predicts Muhammad (Deuteronomy 18:18; John 14:16)."

Fallacy: Proof-Texting (ignoring context).
Rebuttal: Deuteronomy 18:15–18 says the prophet comes "from among your brothers"—fellow Israelites, the same phrase used for Israel's kings (Deuteronomy 17:15)—and Acts 3:22–26 applies it to Jesus. The Helper in John 14:16–17 is the Holy Spirit: invisible, living inside believers, and sent days later at Pentecost (Acts 2:1–4).
Question 45

"Jesus can't be the Messiah—He didn't bring world peace."

Fallacy: False Dilemma (ignoring the two comings).
Rebuttal: The Hebrew Scriptures describe a Messiah who suffers and dies for sin (Isaiah 53:5; Daniel 9:26) and a Messiah who reigns in peace (Isaiah 9:6–7)—fulfilled in two comings. And Daniel 9 requires the Messiah to come before the Temple's destruction in AD 70 (Axioms 15, 18).
Question 46

"The Book of Mormon is another testament of Jesus."

Fallacy: Special Pleading.
Rebuttal: Paul warned against "a different gospel," even if preached by an angel (Galatians 1:8). The Book of Mormon's Nephite cities, steel, and horses have no archaeological support, and Mormon teaching says God was once a man (Joseph Smith's King Follett Discourse, 1844)—not the eternal God of Isaiah 43:10 (Axioms 12, 14).
Question 47

"We are all God—the universe is God."

Fallacy: Self-Refutation.
Rebuttal: If you're God, why did you have to learn that you're God? An all-knowing God can't be ignorant or need enlightenment. Changing, learning, dying beings are creatures, not the Creator (Axiom 5).
Question 48

"Suffering comes from desire, so get rid of desire."

Fallacy: Self-Refutation.
Rebuttal: Desiring to get rid of desire is still a desire. Christianity doesn't erase your deepest longings—it points them to the only One who can satisfy them (Psalm 16:11; John 6:35).
